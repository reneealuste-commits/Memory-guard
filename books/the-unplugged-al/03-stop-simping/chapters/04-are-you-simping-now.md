# How to Tell If You're Simping Right Now

You don't need theory. You need an honest audit of your behavior this week. Not your intentions. Not what you hope she sees. What you actually did when no one was grading you.

## The Quick Check

Answer without excuses:

- Did you text first most of the time?
- Did you change plans you cared about because she seemed off?
- Did you feel relief when she responded and dread when she didn't?
- Did you swallow something you needed to say just to keep the peace?
- Did you make excuses for her behavior while your gut said otherwise?

One yes doesn't decide it. A pattern of yeses does — and so does the dread you'd feel imagining what happens if you just... stopped.

## What Your Body Tells You

Your body knows before your mind admits it:

- Tight chest before you check your phone
- Stomach drop when she's online but not replying
- Difficulty focusing on work or training
- Exhaustion after conversations that should've been easy
- Constant low-grade feeling of being on trial

If being around her — or waiting on her — consistently drains you more than it steadies you, that's signal, not just stress.

## The Reciprocity Check

Last two weeks:

- Who initiated contact more?
- Who adjusted their schedule more?
- Who apologized more?
- Who brought more effort to every conversation?

Healthy connection has rhythm. Not perfectly equal every day, but mutual movement toward each other. Simping is a one-way current. You swim. She floats.

## The Friend Test

If your closest friend described his relationship to you — but it was actually your relationship — would you say "sounds balanced" or "man, you're giving away the store"?

We see others more clearly. Use that clarity on yourself.

## The Boundary Check

- When did you last say no without a long justification?
- When did you last express disagreement without softening it immediately?
- When did you last let her sit with her own reaction instead of rushing to fix it?

If you can't remember, you're managing — her emotions, her perception of you, your fear of loss.

## Your Next Move

If this chapter stung, write down three things you did this week that were simping. Not to punish yourself. To see them clearly.

Seeing it is the first cut. The bleeding stops when you stop pretending the wound isn't there.
