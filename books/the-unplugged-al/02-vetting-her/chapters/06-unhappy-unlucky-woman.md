# The Unhappy & Unlucky Woman

Every job is toxic. Every person is a betrayal. Every ex was the villain. And now you're the next man assigned to fix the weather.

Some women aren't evil. They're just unhappy in a way that never stays contained. The chaos finds new containers — new jobs, new apartments, new men — but the pattern stays identical.

You can't vet clearly if you confuse compassion with recruitment.

## The Pattern

Watch for:

- No stable wins anywhere: career, friendships, health, housing.
- Stories that always cast her as the only innocent party.
- Need intensity that outpaces how long you've known her.
- A crisis cadence that prevents you from having your own life.
- Resistance to any help that requires her to do something differently.

Trauma is real. Your empathy is good. But empathy without limits becomes enlistment.

## A Scene

Four months in: she's between jobs, her car is in the shop, her roommate is a nightmare. You step up — driving her to interviews, spotting her for expenses, listening every night after work.

Three months later: new job she already hates, new roommate situation "going sideways," and a fresh complaint that you don't support her enough.

You are not the solution. You are the latest cast member in a story that has been running for years with the same ending.

## What Happens to You

You become her emotional EMT, financial buffer, and permanent character witness. Your mission shrinks. Friends fade. You feel guilty when you're thriving and she's not.

Love includes support. Chronic carrying without reciprocity is self-erasure.

## Decision Tree

**Is she improving when supported?**
→ Yes: continue, keep your own rhythms intact.
→ No: reduce access. Don't accelerate investment.

**Does she take concrete steps toward change?**
→ Yes: patience is warranted.
→ No: she may be profiting from the instability.

**Does she celebrate your wins?**
→ Yes: good sign.
→ No: you're her contrast. She'll resent your progress eventually.

A woman navigating a brutal year is not the same as a woman who has been "unlucky" for fifteen years with the same behaviors in every situation.

If your success feels like a betrayal to her, you're not her partner. You're her mirror — and it's showing her something she doesn't want to see.

## How to Exit

If the pattern holds: reduce access. Exit without cruelty, without a debate tour, without "let's be friends" that keeps the door open for chaos.

Clear, kind, final: "This dynamic isn't healthy for either of us."

You can't love someone into stability if they profit from the instability. Your presence may delay the crash. It won't become the foundation.
