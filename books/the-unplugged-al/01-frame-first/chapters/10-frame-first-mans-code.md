# The Frame-First Man's Code

You've read the patterns. Principles only become power when they become behavior. This code isn't a summary of what came before — it's the deeper commitments that hold everything together.

Ten rules. Audit your week against them.

## The Ten Rules

**1. You can't lead from fear of her leaving.**
A man who holds frame only when he's sure she'll stay doesn't have frame. He has conditional standards. Real frame holds whether she's approving or packing.

**2. Strength that needs to be seen isn't strength.**
You go to the gym whether she's watching or not. You keep your word when no one's applauding. Frame built for her audience collapses the moment her attention shifts.

**3. A man who needs approval can't give direction.**
You're either navigating or following whoever's most emotional. You can't do both. Pick one and stay there.

**4. Resentment is delayed honesty.**
Every bitter feeling in a relationship is an honest statement you didn't make in time. The buildup isn't her fault alone. Say the true thing now, while it's still a sentence instead of an explosion.

**5. She can't respect what she doesn't know about.**
Hidden standards create invisible violations. If you never tell her your real limits, she's operating on guesses — and you're getting quietly angrier at rules you never stated.

**6. Her happiness is not your job description.**
You're her partner, not her prescription. A man who makes a woman's emotional state his primary responsibility loses himself and eventually fails at the job anyway.

**7. The right relationship expands you.**
If your world has gotten smaller — fewer friends, less work focus, less of you — since she arrived, that's not love. That's absorption. Love doesn't cost you your life.

**8. Silence isn't agreement.**
When you stay quiet to avoid a fight, she hears yes. Say what you mean or expect to live with what she assumed.

**9. Standards require demonstration, not declaration.**
Telling her what you stand for means nothing. Holding it when tested means everything. The only standards that count are the ones you kept when keeping them was uncomfortable.

**10. A man who can leave is a man who chooses to stay.**
You're only genuinely choosing her if you could walk. Staying out of fear, inertia, or sunk cost isn't presence — it's entrapment. She can feel the difference. So can you.

## Audit One Rule This Week

Pick the rule you violated most this week — not the most comfortable one to examine. The one that stings.

**Weekly practice:**
1. Write down one moment you lost frame this week. One sentence, specific.
2. Identify which of the ten rules it broke.
3. Pick one concrete behavior to change before next Sunday.
4. Hold that change even when she pushes back.
5. Repeat until it is reflex, not effort.

Frame isn't a performance. It's what you actually are when no one's watching. Build that man. Everything else follows.
